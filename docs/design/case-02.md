## 1. Executive summary

A feed-ranking change appears to raise click-through rate by about 5% [D13] with p far below 0.001 on the team's dashboard. The dashboard divides all clicks by all impressions and treats every impression as an independent trial. Users were randomised, not impressions; a user's impressions share that user's click propensity, and a few heavy users contribute hundreds of impressions each, so the dashboard's interval is about 1.5 times too narrow and its test rejects a true null about one time in six (measured on the base population of section 6: ratio 1.48, Type I error 16.8% [D1, D2]). Correcting the variance does not change the decision, because the click-through rate is not the quantity the decision is about. The ranking change suppresses low-quality items that heavy scrollers used to see, so impressions per user fall by about 5%, click-through rate rises by about 5%, and clicks per user, the engagement quantity the product lead asked about, is unchanged within its interval (true effect on the base −0.001% [D13]). Three estimands, three lifts, one decision: do not ship as an engagement gain; reframe the change as an efficiency change and decide it on that basis.

The case teaches one thing: a ratio metric has two moving parts, and when the treatment moves the denominator, the ratio can rise while the thing you care about does not. Its second lesson, the one shared with Cases 03 and 04, is that the variance of an estimate must respect the unit that was randomised, not the unit that was measured.

## 2. Business decision

Decision owner: feed product lead.
Action space: ship to 100%, do not ship, or take no decision and investigate. One look only: the case is about the metric, and monitoring policy belongs to Case 01 and Phase 2.
Timeline: enrolment and outcomes share one calendar week, the base population's 7-day split (section 6). A user is eligible at their first logged page view in the week, outcomes are the user's in-views and clicks from then to the end of the week, and an event arrival delay of up to 36 hours puts the analysis clock 36 hours after the week closes. Enrolment and maturity are still two clocks, as in Case 01: users enrolled late in the week have short windows, and the notebook shows outcomes by enrolment day so that nobody reads a late cohort's low count as an effect.
Decision rule (pre-declared), rows checked in order so every outcome maps to one action:

| Order | Condition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The negative-feedback guardrail breaches | Do not ship |
| 2 | Any open decision blocker (section 9), or the must-pass guardrail inconclusive (neither pass nor breach) | No decision; investigate first |
| 3 | Upper bound of the 95% interval on relative lift in clicks per eligible user below 0 | Do not ship: engagement loss |
| 4 | Lower bound on relative lift in clicks per eligible user above +0.5% and the guardrail passes | Ship |
| 5 | Otherwise | Do not ship: the engagement gain is not shown to exceed the +0.5% floor |

The +0.5% threshold is the practical significance floor: the minimum relative engagement lift that justifies the serving cost of the new ranking model. It is a product input from the product lead, and the README says so. The rule is written on clicks per eligible user, not on click-through rate, and the README explains why in section 4's words before any number appears.

Bound convention: every bound the rule reads is an end of the two-sided 95% interval (z = 1.96), so each one-sided reading is at the 2.5% level; the derived figures use the same convention [D5 to D10].

## 3. Unit hierarchy

| Unit | Definition |
|---|---|
| Randomisation unit | In the incident being simulated: user, by deterministic hash of user_id at first eligibility. In the harness: the draw; each arm is a set of independent draws with replacement from the 15,143-user base (section 5); the treatment arm size is Binomial(115,200, 0.5) and the control arm takes the remaining draws, so the two sizes are complementary and a population user may appear in both arms |
| Eligibility unit | User with at least one logged page view in the week (section 6: EB-NeRD logs a page view only when an article is opened) |
| Exposure unit | Impression: one item rendered in the viewport for at least one second. This is finer than the randomisation unit, and that is the case |
| Outcome-observation unit | Impression (click or no click) and user (clicks from first eligibility to the end of the split week, section 2) |
| Analysis unit | Draw (a user in the incident's terms), declared. The dashboard's is the impression, undeclared |
| Clustering unit | Draw: every impression belongs to exactly one draw, and a draw's impressions share that user's propensity |

## 4. Metric, estimand, estimator, decision rule

Three estimands are stated side by side. Each is a legitimate population quantity; only one answers the product lead's question, and the README never writes "the correct CTR".

**E1, decision estimand: clicks per eligible user.** E[C(1)] − E[C(0)], where C is the user's clicks from first eligibility to the end of the split week (section 2), reported as an absolute difference and as a relative lift. Every assigned user is in the denominator; on the base population every user has at least one in-view and at least one click (section 6), so E1 is clicks per reading user. Estimator: difference in user means with a Welch interval; delta-method interval on the log ratio for the relative lift, as in Case 01. Control mean 15.47 clicks per user, standard deviation 17.84, measured on the base [D3]. Sections 1, 4, 5, 7 and 10 quote the base truths of [D13], expected potential outcomes over the 15,143-user population, not single-draw values; the v1.0 expectations are retired there.

**E2, the dashboard's estimand: platform-level click-through rate.** E[ΣC]/E[ΣM] per arm, the ratio of expected totals, an impression-weighted rate. Valid estimator: the ratio of arm sums with a delta-method (linearisation) variance computed at the user level (Deng, Knoblich and Lu 2018): with per-user pairs (C_i, M_i) and R the ratio of sums, Var(R) ≈ [Var(C) − 2R Cov(C, M) + R² Var(M)] / (n M̄²). Cross-checks: a bootstrap that resamples users, and a delete-one-user jackknife, which for a ratio of sums has a closed form from leave-one-out totals and costs one pass. Invalid estimator, kept in the package as `naive_event_rate` so that the case can show it: the pooled two-proportion z-test with n equal to the number of impressions. Control level 0.0906, measured on the base [D3].

**E3: mean user-level click rate** among users with at least one impression, E[C_i/M_i | M_i > 0]. Each user weighs the same, so light users dominate, and the level is 0.1012 against E2's 0.0906, measured on the base (E3/E2 = 1.12). It conditions on a post-treatment event (having an impression); in this design the treatment never removes a user's last impression, so the conditioning set is arm-independent by construction, and the README says this is a simplification. Estimator: Welch on user rates.

**Why they disagree.** E2's lift mixes a numerator effect with a denominator effect. When the treatment removes impressions that carried few clicks, E2 rises, E3 rises less (light users lose almost nothing), and E1 can fall. Measured on the base [D13], the truths in the committed scenario are +5.2% for E2, +4.6% for E3 and −0.001% for E1 (section 10); the v1.0 generator's expectations were +7.4%, +5.5% and +0.03%. Under a pure click lift (scenario C) all three move by the same +2.5%, because τ scales every user's click probability; the estimands separate only when the denominator moves.

**Impressions per eligible user** is reported as a metric in its own right, with its interval, because it is the denominator that moved. Expected −5.0% on the base [D13].

**Guardrail.** Negative-feedback actions ("hide", "not interested") per eligible user, must pass, margin +5% relative, verdict rule as Case 01 (pass if the upper 95% bound is at or below the margin, breach if the lower bound is above it, inconclusive otherwise). In the generator the per-impression negative-feedback rate is 0.5% on in-views outside the old tier, times a multiplier g_i ~ Gamma(2, mean 1) per population user (shared by every draw of that user_id) keyed by a fixed population seed and rescaled to an in-view-weighted mean of 1, and three times higher on old-tier in-views (control level 0.83% per in-view on the base), so the treatment reduces it; its relative standard error, measured on the base, is 1.12% at the section 5 sample size of 57,600 per arm [D8] (the v1.0 figure of 1.2% at 100,000 per arm scaled to 1.58% there, overstating it by 1.41, which is what drove the re-sizing of section 5), so at no true effect it passes its +5% margin with measured probability 0.992 at 57,600 per arm (analytic 0.885 on the v1.0 input) [D9] and under the treatment (−8.9% on the base [D13]) always, in every one of 2,000 replicates. v1.1 quoted "about 99%", which held only at 100,000 per arm and was not re-derived when the sample size fell; section 5 now sizes against that error. The guardrail figures are measured by the harness on the article-age tier of section 6 ([D8], [D9], 4 October 2026). The notebook also re-reads the same replicates at a +3% margin, where the pass rate at no effect is 0.7525 at 57,600 (analytic 0.475 on the v1.0 input) [D9]: a guardrail left unpowered for its margin, which a team then either stalls on or misreads as a pass (Schultzberg, Ankargren and Frånberg 2024). The re-read costs no simulation, because the verdict is a function of an interval the harness already computes.

**Invariants** on the assigned population: assigned counts, device-type mix, subscriber share, pre-period 21-day clicks per user (from `history.parquet`, section 6). The pre-period click column is also the covariate a Phase 2 CUPED case would use.

## 5. Experiment design

Allocation 50/50. The population is the 15,143 users of the base split. Each replicate, and the committed single run, draws 115,200 users in total with replacement, with arm sizes Binomial(115,200, 0.5) so that 57,600 per arm is the expectation and the assignment SRM check of section 8 tests a real allocation, 3.80 draws per population user per arm on average [D11]; the draws of the two arms are independent and their sizes are complementary. The analysis and clustering unit of every estimator is the draw, not the population user: a user drawn three times is three i.i.d. draws from the empirical distribution, and each draw carries its own random stream for the simulated treatment and the guardrail, keyed by draw index, so duplicated users share no treatment noise (section 10).

Sizing rule: an 80% probability that the full ship rule fires (row 4 of section 2: E1's lower bound above the +0.5% floor and the guardrail passes) under the simulated engagement effect of +2.5% (scenario C, τ = 0.025). Both conditions must hold, so the size is set on their joint probability rather than on E1 alone (Schultzberg, Ankargren and Frånberg 2024). The size is 57,600 per arm, set by the harness on 4 October 2026 (decision record v2, section 10). The analytic sizing of v1.2 set 64,100: at the base's coefficient of variation of 1.153 [D4] the relative standard error of E1's lift is 0.644% at 64,100 per arm [D5]; power against zero 0.973 and against the margin 0.874 [D6]; a guardrail pass rate at no effect of 0.916 [D9], scaled from a v1.0 simulated standard error [D8]; product 0.800 [D10], treating the two as independent. Scenario C measured 0.8535 (MC SE 0.0079) at 64,100 on 2 October 2026, outside the pre-registered 0.80 ± 0.025. E1's measured standard error matched the analytic figure to three digits (0.644% [D5]); the guardrail's did not: 1.06% on the base at 64,100 against the 1.50% scaled from the v1.0 input, a factor of 1.41, so the pass rate at no effect is 0.995, not 0.916 [D8, D9]. Straight-line interpolation between the measured rates at 52,500 (0.7575) and 64,100 (0.8535) reaches 0.80 at about 57,635 per arm; 57,600 was run as the single candidate, with the same replicate seeds and estimator as the harness, and measured 0.814 (MC SE 0.0087; 95% interval 0.796 to 0.831), inside the tolerance [D10]. That rate is the acceptance check itself, measured once on the operating-characteristic seed; the full run's scenario C row reproduces it from the same seeds rather than confirming it independently. A second run at oc seed 20261011 (not used before; B = 2,000) measured 0.8215 (MC SE 0.0086), inside the tolerance. The teaching point, carried to the README: a size derived from an unmeasured input drifts once the input is measured, and a pre-registered tolerance with a re-derivation rule turns the drift into a recorded correction rather than a post-hoc adjustment. v1.1 sized on E1 alone and set 52,500 per arm: E1 power against the margin 0.802, guardrail pass 0.855, joint 0.686 analytic, 0.7575 measured [D10]. 52,500 stays in the operating characteristics as a contrast row, the size a team reaches when the guardrail is left out of the power calculation. At +1.0%, power against the margin is 0.110 at 57,600 [D7], which the README states so that an inconclusive E1 is not read as evidence of no effect.

The analytic figure rested on two assumptions, both now tested by the harness: the guardrail standard error (overstated by 1.41, above) and the independence of E1 and the guardrail, which are computed on the same draws. At 57,600 the measured pass rate 0.992 times the measured rate at which E1's lower bound clears the margin, 0.822, gives 0.815 against the joint rate of 0.814; at 64,100, 0.995 × 0.8585 = 0.854 against 0.8535 [D10]: independence holds within Monte Carlo error. The pre-registered rule stands for any future re-sizing: a measured joint ship-rule rate inside 0.80 ± 0.025 confirms the size; outside that band the size is re-derived from the harness before the committed run and the change is recorded in the decision record. The figures assume a fixed horizon and a stable baseline; the manifest's control standard deviation under the tuned δ replaces 17.84 when the data exists.

On E2 the naive interval is not sized; the point of the case is that its width is wrong. Measured on the base (section 6): the delta-method standard error of the E2 lift is 1.48 times the naive binomial one [D1], which gives the naive test a Type I error of 16.8% at nominal 5% (400 A/A splits, MC SE 1.9 points at 16.8%; delta method 4.5%, E1 Welch 5.5%, MC SE 1.1 points at 5%) [D2]. The v1.0 generator's figures were a ratio of 1.55 and about 20%.

## 6. Data provenance and simulation disclosure

Decision of 30 September 2026 (decision record v2, section 8, "Case 02 data decision"): real control, simulated treatment. The base population is the EB-NeRD small variant (Ekstra Bladet News Recommendation Dataset, `train` split: 15,143 users, 232,887 logged page views, 18 to 25 May 2023). The threshold was fixed before the run: a delta-to-naive standard-error ratio at or above 1.4 on A/A splits of the real users adopts EB-NeRD, below it the v1.0 parametric generator stays. The feasibility run (`notes/tools/ebnerd_feasibility.py`, 400 A/A splits, seed 20260929) measured a median ratio of 1.48 (IQR 1.48 to 1.48) for the ratio-of-sums estimator; the naive impression-level test rejected a true null 16.8% of the time (Monte Carlo standard error 1.9 points) against 4.5% for the delta method and 5.5% for the E1 Welch test (about 1.1 points at 5%) [D2]. The headline of section 1 is therefore measured on a real population rather than assumed by a generator. The decision record of 28 September planned Case 02 as semi-synthetic; MovieLens was rejected because it truncates at 20 ratings per user, and EB-NeRD logs every in-view article, including the light-user end.

What is real and what is simulated:

- Control arm, real. Each user's observed 7-day rows. An EB-NeRD behaviour row is one logged page view with its list of in-view articles (`article_ids_inview`) and the article(s) clicked (`article_ids_clicked`). The case's impression is an in-view article (mean 170.8 per user, median 98, p90 420, p99 950, max 2,187, sd 205.2) and the case's click is a clicked in-view (mean 15.5 per user, sd 17.8). The top 1% of users by in-view hold 7.2% of in-views and 6.1% of clicks. Observed estimand levels: E1 = 15.47 clicks per user, E2 = 0.0906 clicks per in-view as a ratio of sums, E3 = 0.1012 mean user-level rate.
- Treatment arm, simulated on the same users' rows by the two-parameter mechanism of section 7. `suppression` δ removes each of a user's low-quality in-views independently with probability δ, never the user's last in-view (the last in-view of the user's latest page view); a removed in-view takes its click with it. `click_lift` τ turns each retained unclicked in-view into a click with probability τ p_i / (1 − p_i), where p_i is the user's observed click rate over retained in-views, so that expected clicks per retained in-view are p_i (1 + τ). Both draws come from the draw's own random stream, keyed by draw index (section 5), so common random numbers hold across scenarios and duplicated users share no treatment noise.
- Quality tier, from article age at impression. EB-NeRD's article totals (`total_inviews`, `total_pageviews`) count only activity "within the first 7 days after it was published" and exist only for articles published after 16 February 2023 (Kruse et al. 2024). On the base, 89.1% of split in-views fall on articles carrying totals, and 84.5% on articles whose 7-day window overlaps the split [D12], so a totals-based proxy is built partly from the split's own clicks. The tier is therefore the oldest tercile of the split's in-views by article age at impression (`impression_time` minus `published_time`), defined for every in-view and free of the evaluation week by construction. Two checks go with it. Validation: on the in-views whose article window closed before the split (4.7% [D12]), the notebook reports the Spearman correlation between each article's median age at impression and its totals proxy, one row per article, and the age terciles the subset covers, a tercile counting as covered when at least 5% of the subset's in-views fall in it; the pre-set reading is that age is supported as a quality proxy if the correlation is at or below −0.3 and at least two terciles are covered, and otherwise the validation is reported as uninformative and the README says the tier rests on the mechanism alone. Contrast: scenario D is rerun with the leaked tier, the bottom tercile of the split's in-views by `total_pageviews / total_inviews`, with in-views on articles without totals assigned by article age as in the main tier, and the shift in the three truths is reported as the sensitivity of the truths to an outcome-informed suppression definition; the contrast changes which in-views the treatment removes, so it measures a change of intervention rather than an isolated leakage bias (Kaufman, Rosset, Perlich and Stitelman 2012). Mechanism check, before the build: the notebook reports the old tier's share of in-views by activity decile and the click rate by tier; the two-stories headline needs the old tier concentrated among heavy users and clicking below E2 [D3], and if either fails the headline is re-derived and the decision record amended before any simulation code is written. Result, 30 September 2026 (A18): the mechanism holds. The old tier's share of in-views rises from 0.167 in the lightest activity decile to 0.385 in the heaviest, and the old tier clicks at 0.0444, 0.49 of E2 [D16]. The validation is uninformative under the pre-set rule (per-article Spearman −0.126 on 1,218 articles; one tercile covered [D16]) and structurally so: every article in the clean subset is more than 168 hours old at impression, against an old-tier cut of 9.2 hours, so the subset can never cover two terciles and its correlation measures variation within the old tier only. The rule stays as pre-set rather than being replaced after the result; the README reports the check as uninformative, says why a leakage-free subset cannot span the proxy's range here, and states that the tier rests on the mechanism. The −0.126 is never reported as weak support for the tier.
- Negative-feedback guardrail, simulated in both arms. EB-NeRD carries no "hide" or "not interested" action, so the `feedback` table keeps the v1.0 mechanism (per-impression rate 0.5%, three times higher on low-quality in-views), and every guardrail figure is labelled simulated. Real guardrail signals (KuaiRand `is_hate` under random exposure; an EB-NeRD quick-bounce proxy from `read_time`) are Phase 2 case sketches after publication (section 16, item 7).
- Pre-period covariate, real. The 21-day click history per user from `history.parquet` supplies `pre_period_clicks_21d`; the v1.0 column `pre_period_impressions_28d` is dropped because the history file carries clicks only.
- Timing. `impression_time` is real; `received_at` adds the 0 to 36 hour pipeline delay identical across arms, as in v1.0.

Two properties of the base population the README discloses. First, every logged user has at least one click in the 7 days (zero-click share 0.000): EB-NeRD logs a page view only when the user opened an article, and the dataset retained only active users, those with 5 to 1,000 clicks over 18 May to 8 June 2023 and at least 5 in-view articles (Kruse et al. 2024, section 2.3.1). The population is therefore "active readers selected on activity in and after the split week", not every user exposed to the feed; E1 is clicks per reading user, and every coverage and Type I error claim in this case is conditional on that selected empirical distribution. Randomisation is within that population, so the A/B comparison is unbiased for it; the v1.0 sentence "including the 8% with no impression" in section 4 no longer applies. Second, E3/E2 is 1.12 against the v1.0 generator's 1.83: heavy readers' rates sit closer to light readers' than the generator assumed. That ratio measures the covariance of rate with volume, not the propensity spread; the 1.48 design effect is between-user propensity variance amplified by squared in-view counts (sd 205 on a mean of 171), and the harness reports the base's user-level rate variance beside it. The sweep in section 10 that varies propensity spread keeps the parametric generator because that spread is not a knob on real data.

Finite population. The 15,143 users are the population. Each replicate of the operating-characteristic harness draws n users per arm with replacement from it, and the truth for every scenario is the finite population's: the mean over its users of the expected potential outcomes under each arm, computed once per scenario and written to the scenario manifest before any replicate runs. Sample sizes above 15,143 per arm (section 5 sets 57,600) draw each user 3.80 times on average [D11]; the estimators then see an i.i.d. sample from the empirical distribution, which is the bootstrap world, and every Case 02 figure quoted at such a sample size says so. The large variant is not used; a one-off feasibility run on it, checking whether the ratio and the coefficient of variation hold at scale, is deferred to 31 October 2026 (section 16, item 5).

Tables the generator writes (long format, so the notebook does real joins with logged row counts): `users`, `assignments`, `impressions`, `clicks`, `feedback` and `telemetry_health`, plus the `truth` audit table, keyed by `draw_id` (the key of `users`, `impressions` and `feedback`, with `user_id` kept as an attribute), with `users` carrying `draw_id, user_id, device_type, is_subscriber, pre_period_clicks_21d` in place of platform, country and the two 28-day columns; `impressions` carrying the real `article_id` in place of `item_id`; `truth` one row per user, audit only, with expected potential in-views and clicks under each arm. Raw data stays under `data/raw/`, untracked.

## 7. Incident mechanism

The new ranking model demotes older, lower-click-rate items, the old tier of section 6; no independent quality label exists, and "low-quality" elsewhere in this case names that tier only. Heavy scrollers, who reach deep into the ranking and see more old articles (if the mechanism check of section 6 confirms it), see fewer of them; light users see almost the same feed. Two things then go wrong on the dashboard, and the notebook separates them.

1. **The interval is wrong.** The dashboard computes clicks over impressions and a binomial standard error with n equal to the impression count. Impressions are not independent trials: they cluster within users whose propensities differ (a user-level standard deviation of 0.6 on the logit scale) and whose counts are heavy-tailed (size-weighted mean impressions per user about 120 against a plain mean of 37). The naive standard error is about 1.5 times too small and the test rejects a true null about one time in six (16.8% measured on the base population; the propensity and count figures in this paragraph are v1.0 generator values).
2. **The estimand does not answer the question.** Fix the variance with the delta method and the click-through rate still rises by 5.2% on the base [D13] with a valid interval that excludes zero. The rate did rise. In-views per user fell by 5.0% and clicks per user moved by −0.001% (base truths [D13]), and the product lead asked about engagement. This is the sharper half of the case: a valid interval on an estimand the decision does not read ships the wrong thing.

Consequences the analysis has to discover, in order: assignment SRM and invariants pass; impressions per user differ by arm, concentrated in the top activity deciles; the naive and delta-method intervals on the same point estimate differ in width by 1.5 times; the three estimands give three lifts; clicks per user is inconclusive under the rule and the guardrail passes.

Wording rule for the README: "This incident is simulated on real reading behaviour: the control arm is observed EB-NeRD activity; the ranking change, its effect and the dashboard are modelled." Neither the change nor the dashboard is described as real.

## 9. Decision blockers

- Assignment issue: no.
- Exposure measurement issue: no logging failure. The exposure unit is finer than the randomisation unit and the treatment changes how many exposures a user gets; this is a metric-construction issue, and the case proposes a new checklist row for it (section 12).
- Outcome logging issue: final-day incompleteness, non-differential, handled by the analysis clock; not a blocker.
- Differential missingness: no.
- Guardrail breach or unmeasurable: no; the guardrail is measured on every assigned user.
- Duration insufficient for delayed outcomes: no, with a 7-day window and a 36-hour maximum delay.
- Metric denominator is a post-treatment quantity: yes, for E2 and E3. This blocks reading either as an engagement estimate; it does not block the experiment, because E1 is available.

## 10. Operating-characteristic validation

B = 2,000 replicates per scenario (v1.2 set 5,000; re-budgeted in v1.3 against measured timings [D14], [D15]) with Monte Carlo standard errors on every rate, run through the Case 01 harness (`simulate_replicates`, `summarise`, paired contrasts under common random numbers, per-unit cache). Six estimators: naive impression-level z-test on E2; ratio of sums with delta method on E2; ratio of sums with user jackknife on E2; ratio of sums with user bootstrap on E2 (scenarios A and D only: standard error from 200 resamples with a normal interval, at 500 replicates per scenario, one bootstrap call on the relative lift per replicate; resamples per replicate set the precision of each bootstrap standard error and replicates per scenario the precision of the coverage estimate, two budgets sized from different error targets, Efron and Tibshirani 1993, chapter 6); Welch on E3; Welch and delta-method log ratio on E1. Each row reports bias against its own estimand, coverage, rejection rate and, for E1, the ship-rule rate.

Scenarios A, C and D are also re-read at a +3% guardrail margin from the same replicates (section 4); no extra simulation runs.

Truths are computed once per scenario from the finite population's expected potential outcomes under (δ, τ) and written to the scenario manifest before any replicate runs (section 6); the v1.0 generator's expectations (+4.8%, +2.9%, −2.4% for B; +2.5% for C; +7.4%, +5.5%, +0.03% for D) are retired to the CHANGELOG, and δ = 0.15 was kept: on the base, scenario D lands E1 in row 5 in 0.962 of replicates [D13], so no re-tuning was needed, and `config.yaml` says so. On E1 and E2, D still equals B compounded with C.

Finite population: every replicate resamples users with replacement from the 15,143-user base, and coverage is measured against the finite population's truth, fixed once per scenario, not against the drawn sample's mean of potential outcomes; the difference between the two is the between-user sampling variance the estimators exist to capture. Arms are drawn independently, the draw is the resampling and clustering unit of `jackknife_variance` and `cluster_bootstrap`, and each draw's random stream is keyed by draw index (section 5). The keyed streams are addressable pseudorandom streams, a vectorised SplitMix64 counter hash (Steele, Lea and Flood 2014) of the seed, the draw index and the use, with counts drawn by a binomial inverse CDF exact up to float64 accumulation, never one generator object per draw: constructing a `numpy.random.Generator` per draw measured 1.85 seconds per replicate at 64,100 per arm, which alone would add about 6 hours of single-core time (about 3 hours on two workers) over the 12,000 scenario replicates [D15].

Sweeps, B = 1,000 per point, run by a separate command, `abf oc case02 --sweeps`, under its own limit [D15]; `abf oc case02` runs the scenario table alone within the core limit. The split keeps the decision-critical figures reproducible in half an hour and discloses the longer run rather than thinning it (decision record v2, section 9, A20). The first two vary parameters the real population does not expose, so they run on the v1.0 parametric generator calibrated to the base (mean in-view count 170.8 and its dispersion fitted to the per-user in-view counts, E2 = 0.0906) and every figure they produce is labelled simulated; they run at 57,600 per arm. The third runs on the base by resampling.

- User heterogeneity, σ of u_i from 0 to 1.0: the naive Type I error against the delta method's. At σ = 0 users still differ through the depth term and the quality share (v1.0 generator figure: naive-to-delta SE ratio about 1.17), so the sweep shows the naive error rising from a non-zero floor; one extra point with the depth term switched off shows the floor at nominal.
- Tail heaviness, NB dispersion r from 0.25 to 4: the naive Type I error, and the delta method's and bootstrap's coverage at the heavy end, where both are known to undercover at small n.
- Sample size, 10,000 to 200,000 per arm, on the base by resampling with replacement (points above 15,143 per arm are in the bootstrap world of section 6): coverage of the delta method and bootstrap, and wall-clock time of the delta method, the jackknife and the bootstrap. The record asked for this comparison; the expected result is that the delta method and the closed-form jackknife cost one pass each and the percentile bootstrap costs 1,000 (timed on 3 replicates per size), and that coverage, compared through the 200-resample normal bootstrap, is the same above about 50,000 users. Its bootstrap coverage comes from the 200-resample normal interval on 200 replicates per size, in scenario A only, (Monte Carlo SE 1.54 points at 95% [D14], coarser than the 0.69 points of the delta method's cells at B = 1,000, and the table says so); the 1,000-resample percentile bootstrap runs on 3 replicates per size for the wall-clock row only. v1.2's 1,000-resample percentile bootstrap at B = 1,000 per size was estimated at about 25,600 seconds single-core on its own, from the per-replicate timings of [D15]. The sweep also reports, at each size, the naive test's Type I error under scenario A and the ship-rule rate under scenario C, with 15,143, 52,500, 57,600 and 64,100 marked. The expected shape of the first is flat near 17% [D2]: the design effect comes from how in-views cluster within users, so more users narrow every interval and never repair the miscalibrated one.

Joint coverage is not reported: the rule reads one parameter.
